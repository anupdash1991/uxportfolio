CASE STUDY 04 / ENTERPRISE INTEGRATIONS

Making integration health visible and actionable.

End-to-end research connected customer workflows to a clearer hierarchy for monitoring integrations, configuring systems, and recovering from failures.

01 / THE QUESTION

Put attention where the work happens.

Integration users needed to understand the health of connected systems and what to do when something failed. Configuration, error context, and operational monitoring competed for attention. The research question was how to organise the experience around the tasks customers needed most.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I owned research strategy, customer discovery, synthesis, prioritisation, and moderated usability. I worked with product and design partners to translate workflow evidence into concrete requirements and tracked recommendations through delivery.

Interaction design contribution

I contributed to information architecture, navigation, and interaction flows for monitoring system status, configuring connected systems, and recovering from errors. I translated workflow evidence into clearer setup states, error-to-source paths, and next-action guidance, then used moderated usability testing to refine these interactions with product and design partners.

02 / MY APPROACH

One research thread.
Connected to decisions.

01

Understand the domain

Stakeholder interviews and platform analysis established the system relationships, constraints, and decisions to make.

02

Diagnose the friction

Customer feedback, support signals, competitive review, and heuristic evaluation helped locate recurring problems.

03

Prioritise the work

Workflow mapping and red-route analysis clarified the tasks that carried the greatest urgency and value.

04

Validate the direction

Moderated usability and an action log connected observed behaviour to design refinements and implementation.

03 / WHAT WE LEARNED

The evidence, and
what it changed.

01

Operational visibility

THE EVIDENCE

Customers needed a joined view of status, activity, and exceptions.

THE DIRECTION

Centralise integration health and surface the items that need attention.

02

Actionable recovery

THE EVIDENCE

Failure messages without source context made users investigate before they could act.

THE DIRECTION

Connect the error summary to its source, explanation, and next action.

03

Configuration clarity

THE EVIDENCE

Setup states and system relationships required too much recall.

THE DIRECTION

Make activation states, relationships, and guidance visible in the workflow.

04

Help at the right moment

THE EVIDENCE

Assistance mattered most at points of uncertainty.

THE DIRECTION

Place concise, relevant guidance close to errors and ambiguous controls.

04 / PRODUCT DIRECTION

A hierarchy organised around attention and action.

Research elevated status monitoring and failure recovery while keeping configuration accessible. Seven research recommendations reached production, including clearer error paths, setup cues, terminology, and mapping. Contextual help remained partly constrained by the host platform.

WHAT I TAKE FORWARD

For complex enterprise systems, first show what requires attention, then explain why, and make the next action clear.

Project scope: research strategy, workflow direction, usability validation, and production follow-through.
