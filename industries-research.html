01 / THE QUESTION

What belongs in the shared product?

Product leaders needed to understand which problems repeated across industries before committing to solutions. The program brought SaaS finance, consumer products, manufacturing and distribution, and healthcare operations into one conversation without flattening their different business contexts and help inform the product roadmap.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I framed the program, coordinated five researchers, identified reasearch methods, owned cross-industry triangulation, and translated the evidence into leadership readouts. I partnered with six product managers to make the opportunity model useful for product direction and prioritisation.

02 / MY APPROACH

One research thread.
Connected to decisions.

01

Connect the evidence

Combine 25+ customer interviews, product feedbacks, previous researches, workflow analysis, and market evidence.

02

Map the work

Place individual signals in the context of end-to-end workflows and mark down spcific patterns.

03

Compare the patterns

Look for recurrence across sources and industries before treating a need as shared.

04

Calibrate the conclusion

Separate strong evidence from directional hypotheses, then carry the distinction into leadership readouts.

03 / WHAT WE LEARNED

The evidence, and
what it changed.

01

One operational view

THE EVIDENCE

Financial and operational context was fragmented across tools.

THE DIRECTION

Unify the overview with role-based paths into detail.

02

Less manual stitching

THE EVIDENCE

Spreadsheets, exports, and duplicate entry bridged gaps in workflows.

THE DIRECTION

Connect handoffs and make status, ownership, and failures visible.

03

Guidance people can trust

THE EVIDENCE

Forecasting and AI needed visible logic and human review.

THE DIRECTION

Explain rationale and provide review controls before action.

04

Exceptions that lead to action

THE EVIDENCE

Passive dashboards did not answer what to do next.

THE DIRECTION

Connect severity, business impact, ownership, and the next action.

04 / PRODUCT DIRECTION

A shared foundation, shaped for each industry.

The opportunity model separated three layers: reusable foundations such as reporting, guided setup, exception handling, and auditability; industry configuration around specific jobs; and governed connections to operational data. It gave teams a common language for roadmap sequencing and reusable patterns.

WHAT I TAKE FORWARD

A repeated pattern is useful only when the team understands where it holds—and where industry context changes it.

One research stream had limited participation. Those signals remained directional and did not drive executive conclusions. Customer names, quotations, private metrics, and proprietary roadmap details are omitted.
